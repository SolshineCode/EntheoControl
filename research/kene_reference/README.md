# Shipibo-Konibo *Kené*, Huni Kuin *Kenê Kuin*, and Yawanawá *Kenê*: Ethnographic Visual Grammar, Ethics & Open-Access Study Reference

> **Purpose:** Accurate ethnographic, geometric, and ethical reference for studying authentic Shipibo-Konibo *kené*, Huni Kuin (Kaxinawá) *kenê kuin*, and Yawanawá *kenê* design systems—replacing a mislabelled generic stepped-labyrinth pattern (now renamed **"Folding Blocks"**) with grounded scholarship, open-licensed museum/archival study photographs, and direct pathways to ethical collaboration or licensing with Indigenous artists and cooperatives.

## 1. Shipibo-Konibo *Kené* — Visual Grammar & Ethnography

The **Shipibo-Konibo** (also spelled Shipibo-Conibo) are a Panoan-speaking Indigenous nation living primarily along the Ucayali River and its tributaries in the Peruvian Amazon, as well as in urban communities such as **Cantagallo** in Lima ([Wikipedia: Shipibo-Conibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipibo-Conibo); [Shipibo Conibo Center](https://www.shipiboconibo.org/)). Their geometric design system—***kené*** (historically spelled *quene* in older ethnography)—is painted, embroidered, woven, incised, and beaded primarily by women onto cotton textiles, ceramics, wooden objects, and human bodies ([Belaunde 2009, Cendoc Chirapaq](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Wikipedia ES: Kené](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken%C3%A9)). On **April 16, 2008**, Peru's Instituto Nacional de Cultura (now the Ministry of Culture) declared *kené* **Patrimonio Cultural de la Nación** via **Resolución Directoral Nacional Nº 540/INC-2008** ([Wikipedia ES: Kené](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken%C3%A9); [Ministerio de Cultura del Perú — Informes y Publicaciones](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/cultura/informes-publicaciones); [Repositorio Institucional del Ministerio de Cultura](https://repositorio.cultura.gob.pe/handle/CULTURA/1401)).

### 1.1 Line Hierarchy (Three-Tier Linear Architecture)

Unlike generic Western maze or labyrinth generators that use a single uniform stroke width, authentic Shipibo-Konibo *kené* operates through a strict **multi-tier line hierarchy** documented by Angelika Gebhart-Sayer (1985), Bruno Illius, and Luisa Elvira Belaunde (2009), and clearly visible in our study corpus (e.g., [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg), [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg), and [`images/artic_85528.jpg`](images/artic_85528.jpg)):

1. **Primary Structural / "Canvas" Framework Lines (*kano* / *ani kené* / *ronin kené*):**
   - Bold, thick principal tracks (often double-outlined or filled bands) are laid down first to partition the surface and establish the macro-topology of the composition ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Wikipedia ES: Kené](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken%C3%A9)).
   - In Shipibo terminology, ***kano*** means "path," "main route," or structural framework (by analogy with the main beam framework of a traditional house), serving as the primary axis linking design regions and guiding the eye—or the healer's spirit—across the visual field ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Wikipedia ES: Kené](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken%C3%A9)).
   - Thick primary form-lines are also termed ***ani kené*** ("large/major design") or ***ronin kené*** when they evoke the long, continuous skin pathways of **Ronin**, the primordial cosmic anaconda ([Gebhart-Sayer 1985, *Journal of Latin American Lore* 11(2): 143–175](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Xapiri Ground](https://www.xapiriground.org/)).
2. **Secondary Parallel / Contour Echo Lines:**
   - Thinner lines trace tightly parallel to both sides of the primary *kano* tracks at a uniform offset, echoing every corner, step, and curve without intersecting the primary stroke. This creates a layered, rhythmic "vibration" or halo around the main path (see the pale yellow/white contour stitching hugging the pink and gold primary lines in [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg) and the paired thin brown lines flanking the bold meanders in [`images/artic_85528.jpg`](images/artic_85528.jpg)).
3. **Tertiary Micro-Fill Lines (*beshe kené*):**
   - Once the primary and secondary frameworks divide the bounded surface into enclosed cells, ultra-fine, intricate auxiliary lines—***beshe kené*** ("minor/fine designs")—fill every remaining negative-space pocket ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Wikipedia ES: Kené](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken%C3%A9)).
   - Rather than dead background hatchings, *beshe kené* are continuous, meandering, crenellated, or stepped micro-paths that follow the boundaries of each cell at consistent spacing (clearly visible in the black mud-dyed filler paths of [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg) and the dense red/green meandering stitches of [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_3.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_3.jpg) and [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg)). Conceptually, they express that no region of the cosmos is empty—everything carries micro-vibrational life.

### 1.2 Spatial Topology: Central Cross, Quincunx, Diamonds & Bounded Fields

* **Cross (*koros*) and Quincunx (Four-Quadrant) Architecture:** Many Shipibo-Konibo compositions organize space around a **central cross**—associated with the **Southern Cross constellation** (*koros*), which dominates the night sky of the Ucayali basin—dividing the field into **four symmetrical quadrants anchored by a central nexus** (a quincunx: four surrounding zones/nodes + one center) ([Gebhart-Sayer 1985](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Wikipedia ES: Kené](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken%C3%A9)). Look closely at [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_3.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_3.jpg), [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg), and [`images/artic_85528.jpg`](images/artic_85528.jpg): crosses and stepped central squares act as rotational hubs and portals connecting cardinal axes.
* **Central Diamonds & "Eye" Nodes (*wero* / *vero*):** Rhombuses, diamonds, and small central squares (often called *wero* or *vero*, "eye" or "seed") punctuate intersections and quadrant centers—related to *wero yoshin* ("spirit of the eye"), the perceptive faculty that links bodily sight to visionary awareness ([Gebhart-Sayer 1985](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)). Compare the diamond chains in the 1865 tunic [`images/smvk_em_1019697.jpg`](images/smvk_em_1019697.jpg) and the diamond eye nodes in [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg) and [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg).
* **Bounded Fields & *Horror Vacui*:** *Kené* is always composed *into* a bounded physical or drawn frame (the border of a *chitonti* skirt panel, the central vertical strip of a *kushma* tunic, or the shoulder/belly zones of a *chomo* ceramic jar). Peter Gow's comparative Western Amazonian design analysis ([Gow 1999, *Piro Designs*, JRAI 5(2): 229–246](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2660695); [Tipití / Trinity Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.trinity.edu/tipiti/vol4/iss1/13/)) highlights how Western Amazonian design compels the viewer's eye through an intense tension between the bounded supporting surface and continuous overlaid linear paths that saturate the field evenly from edge to edge.
* **Continuity ("Lines Never End"):** A core aesthetic principle documented by Gebhart-Sayer and Belaunde is that major *kené* paths should feel **unbroken and endless**—doubling back, turning at stepped right angles or sweeping curves, and continuing smoothly into adjacent zones without abrupt dead ends or chaotic collisions ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Shipibo Conibo Center](https://www.shipiboconibo.org/)). Gebhart-Sayer noted that long meandering tracks are called *ronin kené* precisely because "their ends are difficult to see," mirroring the cosmic anaconda whose patterned skin wraps the entire universe.

### 1.3 Stepped / Rectilinear vs. Curving Lines & Named Motif Types

Shipibo-Konibo artists distinguish formal geometric families—and frequently **combine both rectilinear stepped steps and sweeping curves within the same composition** (visible directly in [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg) and [`images/wikimedia_olinda_silvano_textile.jpg`](images/wikimedia_olinda_silvano_textile.jpg)):

| Shipibo Term | Gloss / Geometric Character | Ethnographic Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ***kené*** (*quene*) | Design, graphic pattern, track | Overarching term for the Shipibo-Konibo visual language applied to textiles, ceramics, wood, and skin ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)). |
| ***kano*** | Path, structural beam/axis | Primary bold framework lines that divide the field and guide spatial flow ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Wikipedia ES: Kené](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken%C3%A9)). |
| ***ani kené*** | Large / primary design | Broad foundational lines contrasted against fine auxiliary fills ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)). |
| ***beshe kené*** | Minor / fine fill design | Delicate secondary/tertiary meandering lines filling negative space along the borders and interior cells ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)). |
| ***maya kené*** (*birish maya kené*) | Curving, turning, swirling, looping design | Flowing curvilinear loops and circular medallions associated with rotational healing motion (*maya bainkin*), whirlpools, and river bends; prominent in masterworks by Sara Flores and Olinda Silvano ([White Cube: Sara Flores](https://www.whitecube.com/artists/sara-flores); [Shipibo Conibo Center](https://www.shipiboconibo.org/)). |
| ***ponte kené*** | Straight / direct rectilinear design | Orthogonal, stepped, grid-like compositions emphasizing clarity, directness, and focus ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); see right panel of [`images/wikimedia_olinda_silvano_textile.jpg`](images/wikimedia_olinda_silvano_textile.jpg)). |
| ***ronin kené*** | Cosmic anaconda design | Long, unbroken, meandering or diamond-scaled tracks embodying Ronin, mother of all designs and rivers ([Gebhart-Sayer 1985](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Wikipedia ES: Kené](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken%C3%A9)). |
| ***koros kené*** | Cross / Southern Cross motif | Symmetrical four-armed crosses dividing compositions into quadrants around an *axis mundi* ([Gebhart-Sayer 1985](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)). |
| ***wero yoshin kené*** (*vero-yushin-quene*) | Eye-spirit design | Curved linear configurations organized around a central cross and diamond eye nodes, linked to soul journeys and visionary perception ([Gebhart-Sayer 1985](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)). |
| ***kewë*** | Embroidered / incised design | Refers to the act and technique of stitching/embroidering or engraving *kené* pathways onto cloth or gourds ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)). |
| ***shama kené*** (*mesko shama kené*) | Composite / layered older style | Complex multi-layered classical design arrangements documented in ethnographic catalogues ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)). |

### 1.4 Relationship to *Icaros* (Sung Designs), Plant Vision & Scholarly Nuance

* **Gebhart-Sayer's "Visual Music" Model:** In her landmark 1985 study *"The Geometric Designs of the Shipibo-Conibo in Ritual Context"* (*Journal of Latin American Lore* 11(2): 143–175), Angelika Gebhart-Sayer documented how Shipibo healers (*onanya*) and women artists conceptualize *kené* and healing songs (*icaros* / *bewá* / *rao bewá*) as synesthetically intertwined: during rituals involving *oni* (ayahuasca) or visionary plant eye-drops such as *piripiri* (*Cyperus* spp., used by women artists to cultivate steady hands and clear mental designs, *xinan*), luminous geometric patterns appear visually and are "sung" onto or into a patient's body to realign disordered energy ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Xapiri Ground](https://www.xapiriground.org/)).
* **Scholarly Refinement (Brabec de Mori & Belaunde):** Ethnomusicologist Bernd Brabec de Mori has cautioned against overly literal New Age claims that every cloth is a direct 1-to-1 musical score readable like Western sheet music, showing instead that the *kené*–*icaro* link is a deep **synesthetic, cosmological, and therapeutic correspondence** rather than a mechanical pitch-to-line cipher. Similarly, Luisa Elvira Belaunde emphasizes that women master *kené* primarily through lifelong apprenticeship, maternal lineage, *piripiri* eye-drops, dreams, and *xinan* (creative thought/memory), wrapping bodies, babies, ceramics, and homes in protective social skin ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)).

### 1.5 Regional, Family & Generational Variation

* *Kené* is **not** a fixed template set; no two traditional cloths are identical. Women inherit stylistic vocabularies from mothers and grandmothers while innovating through personal *xinan* ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)).
* **Historical & Regional Shifts:** Bruno Illius and Donald Lathrap et al. (*"The Roots of the Shipibo Art Style," Journal of Latin American Lore* 11(1), 1985) documented historical transformations between 19th-century wide rectilinear bands on handspun cotton (compare [`images/smvk_em_1019697.jpg`](images/smvk_em_1019697.jpg), collected 1865, and [`images/artic_85528.jpg`](images/artic_85528.jpg), c. 1900) and later 20th/21st-century ultra-dense curvilinear *maya kené* and polychrome embroidery ([Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)). Regional styles vary across the Upper, Middle, and Lower Ucayali, the Pisqui River, **Yarinacocha / San Francisco** (major ceramic and textile centers), **Paoyhan** (birthplace of Olinda Silvano), and the urban **Cantagallo** community in Lima, where artists blend ancestral *kené* with large-scale muralism and contemporary canvas painting ([Shipibo Conibo Center](https://www.shipiboconibo.org/)).

### 1.6 Colour Conventions Across Media

* **Painted / Mud-Dyed Textiles (Traditional Cotton *Tocuyo*):** Unbleached white/cream handspun cotton (*kushma* men's tunics, *chitonti* women's wrap skirts, wall cloths) is painted using a tannin-rich plant extract (such as boiled mahogany bark or *huito* / *Genipa americana*) and fixed with iron-rich river mud (*mapo* / *mahué*). The chemical tannin–iron reaction turns painted lines a permanent **deep black or dark chocolate brown** against **warm cream/off-white** cotton, sometimes paired with **rust-red / mahogany-brown** fields (see [`images/artic_85528.jpg`](images/artic_85528.jpg), [`images/smvk_em_1019697.jpg`](images/smvk_em_1019697.jpg), [`images/smvk_em_1019696.jpg`](images/smvk_em_1019696.jpg), and [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_1.jpg)) ([Xapiri Ground](https://www.xapiriground.org/); [Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411)).
* **Ceramics (*Chomo* beer jars, *Quénti* vessels, *Quenpo* bowls):** Fine white/cream kaolin slip ground painted in **crisp black and terracotta/iron-oxide red** mineral pigments, sealed post-firing with shiny *copal* tree resin (see [`images/wikimedia_masato_chomo_liverpool.jpg`](images/wikimedia_masato_chomo_liverpool.jpg), [`images/wikimedia_quenti_madrid.jpg`](images/wikimedia_quenti_madrid.jpg), [`images/wikimedia_ubc_bowl.jpg`](images/wikimedia_ubc_bowl.jpg), and [`images/smvk_em_1994948.jpg`](images/smvk_em_1994948.jpg)).
* **Contemporary Embroidery (*Kewë*):** Vivid polychrome commercial threads—magenta, turquoise, lime green, golden yellow, violet, red, white—embroidered onto **black, deep indigo, red, or white** cotton ground, often reserving distinct colors for each tier of the line hierarchy (see [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_3.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_3.jpg), [`images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg`](images/wikimedia_shipibo_art_4.jpg), [`images/wikimedia_olinda_silvano_textile.jpg`](images/wikimedia_olinda_silvano_textile.jpg), and [`images/wikimedia_telas_bordadas.jpg`](images/wikimedia_telas_bordadas.jpg)) ([Xapiri Ground](https://www.xapiriground.org/)).
* **Beadwork (*Chaquiras* / Seed Beads):** High-contrast geometric belts, collars, and wristbands combining black, white, red, yellow, orange, blue, and turquoise glass seed beads into diamond and stepped-cross grids (see [`images/wikimedia_vestimenta_mujer.jpg`](images/wikimedia_vestimenta_mujer.jpg)).

> **Why the old VJ pattern ("Folding Blocks") was NOT *Kené*:** A generic uniform-width stepped labyrinth lacks every defining structural feature of Shipibo-Konibo *kené*: (1) no three-tier thick-primary / parallel-secondary / dense-meandering-fill line hierarchy; (2) no central cross/quincunx or diamond-eye (*wero*) rotational organization; (3) no pairing of rectilinear stepped paths with sweeping *maya* curves; and (4) no bounded field saturation with continuous echo contours.

## 2. Huni Kuin (Kaxinawá) *Kenê Kuin* & MAHKU

The **Huni Kuin** (historically called **Kaxinawá** or **Cashinahua**, meaning "True People") are the most populous Indigenous group in Acre, Brazil (living along the Jordão, Tarauacá, Envira, Muru, Breu, and Purus rivers) and also live in eastern Peru ([ISA — Povos Indígenas no Brasil: Huni Kuin](https://pib.socioambiental.org/en/Povo:Huni_Kuin_(Kaxinaw%C3%A1)); [Wikipedia: Huni Kuin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huni_Kuin)). Like the Shipibo-Konibo, they belong to the Panoan linguistic family and practice a sophisticated geometric graphic system called ***kenê kuin*** ("true designs").

### 2.1 Els Lagrou's Ethnographic Framework

Anthropologist **Els Lagrou** (Universidade Federal do Rio de Janeiro) produced the definitive ethnographic scholarship on Huni Kuin visual aesthetics, personhood, and shamanic song-images—most notably ***A fluidez da forma: arte, alteridade e agência em uma sociedade amazônica (Kaxinawá, Acre)*** (Topbooks, 2007), ***Arte Indígena no Brasil*** (2009), and ***"Anaconda-becoming: Huni Kuin image-songs, an Amerindian relational aesthetics"*** (*Horizontes Antropológicos* 24(51): 17–49, 2018; [DOI 10.1590/S0104-71832018000200002](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-71832018000200002)):

* **Cosmological Origin (*Yube*, the Cosmic Boa/Anaconda):** In Huni Kuin myth, *kenê kuin* was taught to women by **Yube**, the primordial anaconda/boa spirit and master of waters and *nixi pae* (ayahuasca). The designs map the markings on the anaconda's skin and structure human perception during visionary states ([Lagrou 2018](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-71832018000200002); [ISA PIB: Huni Kuin](https://pib.socioambiental.org/en/Povo:Huni_Kuin_(Kaxinaw%C3%A1))).
* **Fluidity of Form & Relational Aesthetics:** Because human bodies and souls (*yuxin*) are permeable and mutable, *kenê* applied in *jenipapo* body paint, woven cotton, basketry, and beadwork functions as a protective "second skin" (*dau* / spiritual medicine) that stabilizes identity while mediating contact with non-human beings ([Lagrou 2018](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-71832018000200002)).
* **Image-Songs (*Huni Meka*) & *Anaconda-Becoming*:** During *nixi pae* rituals, men's *huni meka* chants evoke luminous geometric visual webs ("anaconda-becoming"), translating rhythm and acoustic imagery into optical patterns ([Lagrou 2018](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-71832018000200002)).
* **Gendered Mastery:** Traditionally, women are the masters of geometric *kenê* (weaving, basketry, painting, beadwork), learning through ritual seclusion, dietary discipline, and herbal eye-drops (*bawe*), while men's figurative imagery is termed ***dami*** ("figure/transformative image") ([Lagrou 2018](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-71832018000200002); [ISA PIB: Huni Kuin](https://pib.socioambiental.org/en/Povo:Huni_Kuin_(Kaxinaw%C3%A1))).

### 2.2 Named Huni Kuin *Kenê* Motifs & Categories

Verified from Els Lagrou's ethnography and ISA/IPHAN documentation ([Lagrou 2018](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-71832018000200002); [ISA PIB: Huni Kuin](https://pib.socioambiental.org/en/Povo:Huni_Kuin_(Kaxinaw%C3%A1))):

| Huni Kuin Term | Meaning / Gloss | Visual Structure & Cultural Significance |
|---|---|---|
| ***kenê kuin*** | "True designs" | The canonical corpus of Huni Kuin geometric patterns—woven, painted, ceramic, and beaded—defining Huni Kuin cultural identity and personhood. |
| ***txede bedu*** | White-eyed parakeet / *maracanã* eye | **Foundational diamond (*losango*) motif** and the very first pattern taught to young girls learning to weave *kenê*. Mythically linked to the bird that stole cooking fire; symbolizes *bedu yuxin* (liberated spirit-eye / visionary clarity). |
| ***dunu kenê* / *dunu mapu* / *yube kenê*** | Anaconda/boa skin, backbone, core | Interlocking serpentine diamond chains and stepped meander pathways forming the structural spine of major compositions; invokes *Yube*, master of designs. |
| ***inu tae*** | Jaguar's paw | Angular stepped/branched motif associated with predatory acuity and strength; often nested with *txede bedu* as ***inu tae txede bedu*** ("jaguar's paw with parakeet's eye"). |
| ***kapë* / *kapë hina*** | Caiman / caiman's tail | Stepped zigzag / serrated diamond scales evoking the armored back and tail of the river caiman. |
| ***nawan kenê*** | "Stranger's / foreigner's design" | Designs incorporating alterity (*nawa* = non-Huni Kuin / outsider / neighboring peoples), demonstrating how the Huni Kuin graphic grammar actively domesticates external forms. |
| ***kashi*** | Moon / bat associations | Tied to lunar cycles, moiety naming, and paired diagonal motifs in ritual graphics. |

### 2.3 Diagonal Interlocking Stepped Geometry & *Miçanga* Beadwork

* **Diagonal Lattices & Figure-Ground Ambiguity:** Compared to Shipibo-Konibo *kené* (which often mixes fluid *maya* curves with orthogonal stepped tracks and ultra-fine tertiary fills), Huni Kuin *kenê kuin* is intensely **diagonal, interlocking, and figure-ground reversible**—rooted in the physical grid of **cotton twill weaving, diagonal twill basketry, and glass seed-bead loom/peyote work** ([Lagrou 2018](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-71832018000200002); [ISA PIB: Huni Kuin](https://pib.socioambiental.org/en/Povo:Huni_Kuin_(Kaxinaw%C3%A1))). Positive and negative shapes lock together with equal visual weight (clearly visible in the black-and-white woven cap in [`images/wikimedia_hunikuin_katxanawa.jpg`](images/wikimedia_hunikuin_katxanawa.jpg), the clay vessels [`images/smvk_vkm_14630.jpg`](images/smvk_vkm_14630.jpg)–[`images/smvk_vkm_14636.jpg`](images/smvk_vkm_14636.jpg), and the *miçanga* headbands and chest plates worn by MAHKU artists in [`images/wikimedia_mahku_venice.jpg`](images/wikimedia_mahku_venice.jpg)).
* ***Miçanga* (Seed Beadwork) Color Usage:** Contemporary Huni Kuin women translate *kenê kuin* into durable glass seed-bead (*miçanga*) headbands, necklaces, cuffs, and ceremonial chest panels. Color palettes rely on **high-contrast chromatic pairings**—especially **black, white, crimson red, golden yellow, orange, cobalt blue, turquoise, and emerald green**—where alternating light/dark bead bands accentuate stepped diagonal zigzags, nested *txede bedu* diamonds, and interlocking Greek-key/serpent meanders (see close-up beadwork in [`images/wikimedia_mahku_venice.jpg`](images/wikimedia_mahku_venice.jpg)).
* **Heritage Safeguarding (Brazil):** Brazil's heritage agency **IPHAN** (*Instituto do Patrimônio Histórico e Artístico Nacional*) has worked with Huni Kuin communities on documenting and safeguarding *Kenê Kuĩ* as intangible cultural heritage alongside collective intellectual property debates ([ISA PIB: Huni Kuin](https://pib.socioambiental.org/en/Povo:Huni_Kuin_(Kaxinaw%C3%A1))).

### 2.4 MAHKU (*Movimento dos Artistas Huni Kuin*)

* **Origins & Mission:** Formally established in **2013** out of drawing and research workshops led by spiritual leader and researcher **Ibã Huni Kuin** (and his sons Acelino/Bane, Cleiber, and collaborators) in Acre, **MAHKU** is an Indigenous artists' collective translating sacred *huni meka* chants and *nixi pae* visions (*mirações*) into vibrant large-scale paintings, murals, and site-specific installations ([MASP: *MAHKU: Mirações*](https://masp.org.br/exposicoes/mahku-miracoes); [Fondation Cartier](https://www.fondationcartier.com/en)).
* **Political & Territorial Economy:** MAHKU operates under the guiding motto ***"Vende tela, compra terra"*** ("Sell a canvas, buy land"), using contemporary art sales and commissions to purchase and reforest ancestral land in Acre and support community autonomy ([MASP](https://masp.org.br/exposicoes/mahku-miracoes); [Carmo Johnson Projects](https://www.carmojohnsonprojects.com/)).
* **Global Recognition:** MAHKU has held major solo exhibitions at the **Museu de Arte de São Paulo (MASP)** (*MAHKU: Mirações*, 2023; [MASP](https://masp.org.br/exposicoes/mahku-miracoes)), collaborated with the **Fondation Cartier pour l'art contemporain** ([Fondation Cartier](https://www.fondationcartier.com/en)), and painted the monumental facade mural of the Central Pavilion at the **60th Venice Biennale (2024)** (see [`images/wikimedia_mahku_venice.jpg`](images/wikimedia_mahku_venice.jpg)).

## 3. Yawanawá *Kenê* Design Notes

The **Yawanawá** ("People of the White-Lipped Peccary," *yawa* = peccary, *nawa* = people) are a Panoan-speaking Indigenous people inhabiting the **Terra Indígena Rio Gregório** in Acre, Brazil ([ISA PIB: Yawanawá](https://pib.socioambiental.org/en/Povo:Yawanaw%C3%A1)). Following a major late-20th-century cultural and spiritual revitalization—including the historic ordination of female shamans such as Putany and Hushahu—Yawanawá *kenê* body painting and beadwork have become central pillars of cultural identity and women-led economic autonomy ([ISA PIB: Yawanawá](https://pib.socioambiental.org/en/Povo:Yawanaw%C3%A1)).

* **Cosmology & Transmission:** As among the Shipibo-Konibo and Huni Kuin, Yawanawá *kenê* are received through ancestral transmission and visionary work with *uni* (ayahuasca), with women serving as the primary keepers and beadwork weavers.
* **Key Documented Motifs:**
  - **Boa Constrictor / Anaconda (*Yube* / *Ronin*):** Interlocking diamond and scale matrices symbolizing ancestral teaching, healing, and spiritual protection.
  - **Butterfly (*Awavená*):** Wing-like symmetrical geometric motifs strongly associated with spiritual transformation, feminine beauty, and the Yawanawá cultural renaissance; frequently painted across the cheeks and around the eyes in black *jenipapo* (visible on faces in [`images/wikimedia_yawanawa_festival_4031316999.jpg`](images/wikimedia_yawanawa_festival_4031316999.jpg) and [`images/wikimedia_yawanawa_uniao.jpg`](images/wikimedia_yawanawa_uniao.jpg)).
  - **Fishbone / Scales (*Washushaká*):** Sequential, rhythmic chevron and herringbone lattices referencing river abundance and ecological balance.
* **Festivals & Beadwork (*Miçanga*):** During the annual **Festival Yawá** and **Mariri** celebrations along the Rio Gregório, communities paint bodies collectively with **black *jenipapo*** (*Genipa americana*) and **scarlet *urucum*** (*Bixa orellana*) and wear high-contrast, multi-strand glass seed-bead (*miçanga*) cuffs, chest collars, bandoliers, and headbands combining bold primary/secondary colors (red, yellow, orange, blue, green, black, white) in stepped diamond grids (see [`images/wikimedia_yawanawa_mariri.jpg`](images/wikimedia_yawanawa_mariri.jpg), [`images/wikimedia_yawanawa_festival_4031316999.jpg`](images/wikimedia_yawanawa_festival_4031316999.jpg), and [`images/wikimedia_yawanawa_uniao.jpg`](images/wikimedia_yawanawa_uniao.jpg)).

## 4. Ethics, Indigenous Rights & Artist Collectives for Collaboration / Licensing

### 4.1 Legal & Heritage Protections

* **Peru — Cultural Heritage of the Nation (2008):** **Resolución Directoral Nacional Nº 540/INC-2008** (April 16, 2008), advanced through the joint efforts of the Shipibo-Konibo artists' collective *Barin Bababo* ("Children of the Sun") and anthropologists including Luisa Elvira Belaunde, formally recognizes *kené* as **Patrimonio Cultural de la Nación**—affirming it as a living cosmological, scientific, and identity-bearing heritage rather than generic "public domain ornament" ([Wikipedia ES: Kené](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken%C3%A9); [Belaunde 2009](https://cendoc.chirapaq.org.pe/items/show/10411); [Ministerio de Cultura del Perú](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/cultura/informes-publicaciones)).
* **Peru — Law No. 27811 (2002, INDECOPI):** Peru's pioneering *Protection Regime for the Collective Knowledge of Indigenous Peoples Derived from Biological Resources* ([WIPO Lex — Law No. 27811](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/3420)) establishes inalienable, imprescriptible collective Indigenous rights, Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC), and benefit-sharing overseen by INDECOPI. While written primarily around biodiversity-linked traditional knowledge, Indigenous legal advocates—such as the **IP² Foundation** ([ipsquared.foundation](https://www.ipsquared.foundation/))—actively cite it alongside *sui generis* IP frameworks to demand legally binding consent and compensation for commercial uses of *kené*, noting that Western individual-author copyright leaves collective ancestral art vulnerable to extraction.

### 4.2 Community Positions & Appropriation Controversies

* **Why Uncredited Outsider Reproduction Is Harm:** Both Shipibo-Konibo and Huni Kuin leaders consistently stress that *kené* / *kenê* is inseparable from Indigenous land sovereignty, forest protection, plant medicine, and women's livelihoods ([Shipibo Conibo Center](https://www.shipiboconibo.org/); [IP² Foundation](https://www.ipsquared.foundation/)). When non-Indigenous fashion brands, psychedelic festivals, or digital/VJ creators extract *kené* aesthetics without consent, credit, or compensation—or label generic procedural graphics "Shipibo kene"—it erases living Indigenous authors and redirects economic value away from the communities defending the Amazon.
* **The November 2024 Anís Samanez Controversy (Lima):** Public debate erupted across Peru in late November 2024 after fashion designer Anís Samanez complained onstage at Lima's *Orígenes* sustainability event that Shipibo-Konibo women artisans demanded financial payment rather than sharing *kené* for free in exchange for her "design expertise" (saying *"I'm Peruvian too"*), with a senior *Vogue Latin America* editor defending her. Indigenous leaders, artists, and the Peruvian public roundly condemned the remarks as colonial appropriation; retail partners pulled her line and both speakers apologized. The controversy crystallized a clear community standard: **heritage is not free raw material for outsiders—engagement requires paid, consensual partnership on Indigenous terms.**

### 4.3 What Respectful Use Looks Like for a Live Projection / VJ Engine

1. **Never mislabel generic generative code as authentic *kené*:** Keeping the renamed procedural pattern **"Folding Blocks"** (and keeping any non-Indigenous mathematical geometry clearly labelled as abstract/procedural) is the right baseline.
2. **Study for literacy, not extraction:** Use the ethnographic grammar and open-licensed museum photos in this dossier to *understand* how real Panoan design works—not to train a scrape-and-clone generator marketed as "Shipibo" or "Huni Kuin."
3. **Commission, license, or co-create for real *kené* visuals:** If you want genuine Shipibo-Konibo or Huni Kuin visual projections in live shows:
   - **Commission original vector/painted/embroidered artworks or animated loops** directly from a Shipibo-Konibo or Huni Kuin artist or cooperative under a written licensing agreement specifying scope (live performance, commercial/non-commercial, duration), attribution, and **upfront fee + revenue sharing**.
   - **Credit explicitly** in show bills, projection credits, and software documentation (naming the specific artist, their community/nation, and their link—never generic "Amazonian tribal visuals").
   - **Avoid sacred-claim misrepresentation:** Never imply that projected software visuals carry ceremonial healing power (*icaros*/*huni meka*) or replace living Indigenous practitioners.
